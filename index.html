How they're made

Four passes

Every environment goes through the same four passes, and the camera comes first. Nothing is built that the lens won't see.

  1. 01

    Block out

    Grey shapes and a camera. The shots decide the layout, and the layout decides what gets built.

  2. 02

    Dress

    Rock, foliage, props and decals, added until the set holds up at the distance the lens sees it from.

  3. 03

    Light

    Sun, sky, fog and practical lights. This is the pass that makes a place feel like a time of day.

  4. 04

    Shoot & grade

    Camera moves in Sequencer, rendered at 4K, then graded and cut to music.

2026 · Desktop companion

Tidewrack

A coastline at the bottom of your screen.

A strip of beach sits under your other windows while you work. The tide runs on a real clock and leaves things on the sand: shells, sea glass, bottles, bone. Clean them, identify them, and fill a cabinet. A pet you design dives for more. Leave a spare find out and an animal comes for it.

No streaks, no daily rewards, nothing that punishes you for closing it. It is a calm game in a genre built on making you come back.

Unity 6, C#, transparent click-through window

6 coasts, 54 finds, 12 visitors, storms as prestige

2026 · Management horror

Night Shift

Don't let him go home.

Udin works the overnight desk at a lost property office. You are not Udin. You can only send him to things: the phone, the fuse box, the hatch, the back door. Everything you send him to do frightens him, and his nerve only comes back while he sits at his desk doing nothing.

Two or three things want him at once, and the far corner really is far. The game is choosing what to let go.

Wildcode

Raise one partner. It becomes what you made it.

Raise a single creature, called a Wild, on an island where the data shows through the grass. Feed it, train it, praise it or scold it, then command it in side-on battles. How you raise it decides what it grows into, across a branching tree of seven forms.

Inspired by the raise-one-partner games of the late nineties, without the part where your creature dies.

04 / About

DesignD Studio makes places to look at in Unreal Engine, and games to sit in with Unity.

The Unreal work is environments made to be filmed: laid out for the camera, dressed, lit, shot in Sequencer and cut to music. Each one is a short film, not a turntable.

The games are small on purpose. Each one is finished and released, and each is as calm or as tense as it set out to be: a beach that lives at the bottom of your screen, a night shift you have to keep someone through, a jar that fills with rain.

Everything is made in one studio, from the models and the levels to the code, sound, edits and trailers.
